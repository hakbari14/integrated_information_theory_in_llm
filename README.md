The pursuit of Artificial General Intelligence(AGI) is a central goal in Large Language Model(LLM) development, in which consciousness-like processing could serve as a key facilitator. While current language models are not conscious, they exhibit behaviors analogous to certain aspects of consciousness. Here, we adopt \textbf{I}ntegrated \textbf{I}nformation \textbf{T}heory(IIT) as a quantitative framework of assessing consciousness of a system. This allows maximization of such metrics through Reinforcement learning(RL). In this theory, a system is viewed as causal evolution of states, and the theory provides a formal, axiom-based mathematical framework for quantifying consciousness. Inspired by its postulates, we formulate three novel reward functions to evaluate some or all properties associated with consciousness. Empirically, we find that the \emph{intrinsic information reward}, comparable to the baselines, leads to more concise text generation (up to \textbf{35\%}) while still yielding marginal improvements in accuracy, enhancing faithfulness. The proposed framework offers significant practical advantages: it is conceptually simple, computationally efficient, requires no external data or auxiliary models, and leverages a general, capability-driven signal rather than task-specific heuristics.
